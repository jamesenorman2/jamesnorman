Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025

Martyn’s Law readiness check

Named after Martyn Hett, who was killed in the Manchester Arena attack in 2017. The law asks venues and events to be ready to protect people. Answer four questions to see roughly where you stand. It takes a minute.

1. Is this a building or venue, or a one-off event?
2. Is it used for one of the activities the Act lists?

The Act lists the uses that count. They include, for example, shops, restaurants and bars, entertainment and leisure venues, sports grounds, places of worship, visitor attractions, health and education settings. Check the full list in the Act or the Home Office guidance.

3. Is it mainly a place of worship?
4. How many people could reasonably be there at the same time?

Think about your busiest realistic moment, not your licensed capacity on paper.

5. Is entry controlled, for example by tickets, wristbands or checks at the door?

The law in one minute

200–799 people

Standard tier

Tell the regulator, and have simple, practised procedures: evacuation, invacuation, lockdown and how you’ll warn people. Focused on people and planning, not expensive kit.

800+ people, and qualifying events

Enhanced tier

Everything in the standard tier, plus public protection measures that reduce vulnerability, documented and shared with the regulator, with a designated senior individual responsible.

Regulator

The SIA

The Security Industry Authority regulates the new duties, with an implementation period of at least two years after Royal Assent in April 2025 before they apply.
